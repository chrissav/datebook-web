Datebook

Privacy Policy

Last updated 7 August 2026

Datebook is a private app for two people to plan dates together and keep a record of the ones they went on. This policy describes exactly what it stores, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

What Datebook collects

  • Your email address. Used to sign you in and to address an invitation to someone. Nothing else.
  • Your name, chosen emoji, an optional photo, and an optional line about yourself. Shown to the person you’re dating. Nobody else can see it.
  • The date ideas, plans and dates you record — titles, places, addresses, times, your votes, whether you’d go back somewhere, your notes, and any photos you attach.
  • Approximate location, only while you’re using the map, and only if you allow it. It centres the map and works out how far away a place is. Your location is never recorded, never stored, and never sent anywhere.

Datebook has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs that watch what you do.

Who can see it

The person you’re dating, and nobody else. This is enforced by the database itself, not just by the app: every table has row-level security policies that make another couple’s data unreadable, and photos live in private storage reachable only through short-lived signed links.

Votes are a specific case. Your votes on a date idea are invisible to your partner until you have both finished voting — again enforced by the database, so a reveal can’t happen early even by mistake.

Nobody at Datebook reads your dates, notes or photos. Nothing is sold, shared, or given to advertisers, because there aren’t any.

Where it’s stored

Data is held in Supabase, which hosts the database and file storage. Sign-in codes and invitation emails are delivered by Brevo. Both act only as processors — they store and transmit the data so the app can work, and use it for nothing else.

Searching for a place sends your search text and the map area you’re looking at to OpenStreetMap, or to Google Places instead where that’s configured. Your identity and your Datebook are not sent, and neither is told who is searching.

Place data from OpenStreetMap is © OpenStreetMap contributors, used under the Open Database Licence.

Deleting it

Settings → Delete my account, inside the app. It removes your profile, your photos, your votes, your reviews and your notes, permanently and without a waiting period.

One thing to know: plans you made with someone stay with them. If you created a date idea you shared, deleting your account leaves it in their Datebook rather than removing something from someone who didn’t ask for it to go. Anything that is only yours is deleted outright.

Signing out, by contrast, leaves everything where it is so you can come back.

Children

Datebook isn’t directed at children and shouldn’t be used by anyone under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Anything that materially affects what’s collected or who can see it will be announced in the app before it takes effect.
